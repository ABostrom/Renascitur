### _The Unveiling of the Deep Line_

The air inside the **Grand Concourse of Thelassia Station** is thick with steam, sweat, and anticipation. From towering platforms carved straight into the obsidian bedrock, citizens from every walk of life press forward. Ferrun engineers in brass-trimmed coats, dwarven runepriests bearing hammers of office, guildmasters with braids woven in silver wire—all have gathered beneath the immense vaulted ceiling, hundreds of feet above.

The walls are smooth-cut stone, burnished to a mirror sheen, with veins of copper and electrum channelling soft pulses of light that arc across the ceiling like veins of the earth itself. Inlaid along the arches are names—cities, holds, and lost outposts of every dwarven clan, glowing with pale gold script: [[Draumhavn]], [[Thundrakar]], [[Magnus’ Rest]], [[Uftine]]. Murmured prayers are whispered to the stone.

Then, silence.

A single **chime bell** tolls, not from a tower, but from the heart of the line itself.

The **massive gate** at the far end of the platform—fortified, rune-sealed, and untouched since its construction—begins to unlock. A groaning rumble echoes through the cavern as gears the size of wagons rotate and slot into place. Steam hisses. The scent of iron and oil fills the air.

And then it appears.

**The Deep Line Engine** emerges from the tunnel with a thunderous _clang_, its brakes whining like a hymn of steel. It is no tribute to any one people—it is **pragmatic, powerful, and precise**. Every bolt, every plate, every articulated joint speaks of unity through mastery. There is no ornamentation beyond purpose; every component is beautiful because it is _right_.

The engine is a sleek, armoured monolith of matte black steel, layered like overlapping shields. Runes of locomotion and shielding glow faintly at junction points, subtle and sparing. The head car gleams with polished metal and riveted glass domes. Behind it trail cars of various sizes—modular, swappable, and built to serve many roles: freight, passengers, diplomacy, and defence.

The platform responds in kind. Mechanisms built into the floor unfold like clockwork arms, connecting seamlessly with the engine’s coupling points. Hidden rune-circuits flare to life along the edge of the rails. For a moment, the station breathes.

A dwarven official, clad in simple but formal garb, steps forward to a raised dais.

> “The Line is open. From this station, a bridge is laid—not of words, but of _work._”  
> “Let this train run not just on iron and fire, but on the will of all kin. From mountain to coast. From forge to wave. From clan to kin.”

A pause. Then:

> “Let it _run_.”

Thunderous applause. Sirens blare in joy. The concourse erupts into movement—cheers, music, and the clatter of footsteps as workers surge forward to see the train up close.

Above, on a ledge etched with centuries of stone-carved names, a new line begins to glow, letter by letter:

> **DEEP LINE OPENED — TO DRAUMHAVN — 4th Era, 256**


### _A Carriage for Legends_

As the cheering fades and the great engine exhales steam in idle pulses, a dwarven stationmaster approaches the party. He’s clad in a waistcoat reinforced with fine chain, a ledger scroll tucked under one arm, and a monocle bound to his head by a leather strap and brass housing.

He bows low, his voice clipped and formal.

> “Honored guests of Thelassia and bound for Draumhavn—this way, if you please.”

He leads them past the gawking crowds, down a reserved walkway flanked with brass pylons and glowing rope-light. Other passengers, even dignitaries, step aside, recognizing the summons. Some nod with respect; others whisper.

Ahead, nestled just behind the main engine and sealed with a sigil-lock of molten silver, waits their carriage.

It is unlike the utilitarian cars behind it.

This is no cargo hauler or officer's bunkroom. This is **a relic of prestige and preparation**—a long, reinforced carriage of burnished ironwood and blackened steel, rimmed in dwarven brass and framed with shock-dampening pistons. Wide, arched windows of crystalglass glow faintly with embedded runes of pressure and privacy, set within reinforced frames that bear both **Ferrun** and **clanless** sigils—an intentional statement of neutrality and honor.

The carriage door opens with a hiss of arcane seals disengaging, and a low chime plays—a tonal phrase familiar to dwarves: **"By iron and effort, you are welcome."**

Inside, warm lamplight spills onto finely grained oak floors, bolted and polished, overlaid with dark green carpets in geometric patterns. The walls are lined in brushed metal with insets of volcanic glass and stone carvings—landmarks from across the dwarven world, from the basalt cliffs of Draumhavn to the lost towers of Runehart.

There are six heavy leather seats—wide enough for a goliath, contoured for armor, and bracketed with adjustable restraints for sudden stops. Between them sit inlaid tables with built-in map drawers and a sliding chessboard made of ferrum and slate. A small alchemical minibar is tucked to one side, beside a rune-cooled cabinet bearing refreshments and ration crates branded with the seal of Thelassia.

One end of the carriage is sealed by a steel iris door leading to a **private armory compartment**, sized precisely for gear storage and weapon access in emergencies. The other end houses a compact **briefing nook**, with a fold-out projector crystal and scroll rack.

A carved plaque near the door reads:

> _“For those who walk ahead of history.  
> May the stone remember your steps.”_

As the party steps inside, the outer door seals behind them with a reassuring _clang_. Outside, steam jets hiss. The rails beneath begin to tremble. The Deep Line is about to move.


### _The Descent to [[Draumhavn]]_

The first day is all **excitement and wonder**.

The moment the Deep Line pulls away from the Grand Concourse, it carries with it the eyes of a hundred spectators and the weight of a thousand years of dwarven isolation. The city of [[Thelassia]] falls behind. Conversation in the private carriage is animated, filled with speculation, strategy, and laughter.

By the second hour, the party has settled into the rhythm of the rails—**a deep, constant thrum**, like a drum echoing in the bones.

---

### ⏳ _Days Pass in Stone and Steam_

Time blurs in the world beneath. No sun marks the passing hours. Only the **changing hue of the cabin lights**, which dim and brighten in artificial cycles, gives any indication that day has turned to night.

Each morning, trays of food are delivered with mechanical punctuality by a brass-clad attendant golem—simple dwarven fare, but hearty: thick oatcakes, salted meat strips, cured mushrooms, and steaming mugs of caf-root or brewroot ale. On the third day, a loaf of fresh-baked darkbread appears, crust still warm. Someone in Thelassia must have slipped in a care package.

The private armory and briefing area serve the party well—tinkering with gear, etching new runes, or reviewing maps of the coast and projected politics in Draumhavn. Occasionally, they step into the connector corridor to look down the length of the train, catching glimpses of the crew or other passengers in adjacent cars—merchants, scholars, and dwarves with wary, watchful eyes.

At night, the seats fold down into comfortable bunks, and thick woolen blankets keep the deep-chill at bay. The rock groans around them as the pressure increases, and the temperature drops. The only constant is the heartbeat of the rails.

---

### ⚒️ _The Depths Grow Stranger_

By the fourth day, the **architecture of the tunnels begins to shift**. The rail line no longer cuts through hewn stone alone. Now it winds through **forgotten caverns**, immense and echoing, lit by flickering soul-lanterns or clusters of bioluminescent lichen. Broken pillars jut from collapsed bridges. Echoes carry the memory of something large, moving far in the dark.

On the fifth day, the train slows unexpectedly. A maintenance team disembarks to clear rubble from a minor rockfall. The party watches from within as silent figures in stone-filtered suits haul debris away, their breath visible in the cold air. For a moment, they are reminded: this route is ancient, and not all of it is safe.

---

### 🌊 _The Last Mile_

At last, on the **seventh day**, the Deep Line begins its final descent.

The tunnel widens. The rock gives way to walls lined in deep-sunk obsidian, flecked with sea-salt crystals and pitted with old barnacle scars. Then, the wall beside the carriage **vanishes**, replaced by towering **[[Frost Diamond|Frostglass]] panels**, like windows into the soul of the world.

The sea.

Not near it—**within it**.

Darkness stretches in all directions, cut only by shafts of filtered light and the flickering silhouettes of abyssal creatures. Immense things pass in the distance—whale-sized shadows with bioluminescent frills, slow as gods. Here and there, dwarven beacon-torches mounted outside the tunnel cast narrow cones of light into the depths.

And then they see it.

Far beyond the curve of the seafloor, in the very heart of the ocean’s void, lies the [[The Wakened Trench]].

It pulses faintly—once, twice—**a violet star** sunken in black water. No bottom is visible. No wall, no edge. Just a presence. A wound in the world.

Even from this distance, it draws the eye. No one speaks. The silence in the carriage is total.

---

### 🛡️ _The Sea Gates of Draumhavn_

Breaking that silence is the hiss of pressure seals and the low _clang_ of iron teeth disengaging.

Ahead, the **Sea Gates** begin to open—massive structures of interlocking brass and basalt plates, each etched with ancient runes of containment and vigilance. Spiked towers rotate to face the incoming train. Chain-guided locks unwind. The tunnel opens like a mouth.

The Deep Line enters at a crawl. A final runic flare pulses along the ceiling, and the protective enchantments around the train flare a moment brighter.

And then—

Beyond the gates, the tunnel curves upward. Light bursts into the windows as the train emerges into a **massive dome** carved from a hollowed-out undersea mountain. Welcome runes pulse along the walls. Stone platforms rise on hydraulics. And overhead, barely visible through layers of enchanted crystal, the ocean continues to press down—held at bay by **will, stone, and war-forged engineering**.

The platform rises to meet them. The brakes engage.

The train hisses to a halt.

**Draumhavn has been reached.**

### _Arrival in Draumhavn_

The doors to the private carriage _hiss open_, and a wave of brine-scented air rolls in—cold, heavy, and clean, like a forge washed in seawater.

A broad platform of **polished basalt and reinforced bronze** awaits the party, rising seamlessly from the bedrock. Above, a web of **arcane wards and shimmering forcefields** holds the crushing ocean at bay, casting everything in shifting hues of deep blue and emerald green. Fish shadows swim far above, their movement flickering like candlelight across the reinforced dome.

As the adventurers step down onto the platform, they are met by an honor guard: **five dwarves in sea-bronze armor**, engraved with neutral sigils—not clan markings, but the emblem of the **Harbor Assembly**, the ruling council of Draumhavn. Their pauldrons are shaped like wave-crests; each helmet bears a visor like a diving helm, folded up to reveal weathered faces and salt-streaked braids.

The leader steps forward and places a closed fist over his heart.

> “Welcome, travelers. By command of the Assembly, and in recognition of your service to dwarvenkind, you are granted safe passage and honored status within the walls of Draumhavn. You walk on stone sealed in oath and salt. We bid you enter.”

Behind them, the city awaits.

### _The Heart of Draumhavn_

The station spills into a massive concourse—**cathedral-like in scale**, but shaped more by function than faith. Ramps and lifts rise in coiled spirals around thick columns, directing foot traffic through multi-tiered levels. Dwarves bustle in every direction: laborers unloading goods from the Deep Line, artisans hauling carts of sea-glass and rune-etched coral, diplomats flanked by envoys and message hawks.

Everywhere, the walls gleam with **moisture-slick stone**, inlaid with rivulets of constantly flowing water, channelled through aqueducts and **runic plumbing**. Above, the ceiling is a marvel: a dome of interlocked glass panes and metal ribs, offering an unbroken view of the **undersea world** beyond. Faint sunlight filters through leagues of water, lending everything a blue-green haze.

You catch glimpses of **submersible ferries** drifting beyond the city dome, like airships beneath the sea, and massive **harpoon turrets** slowly tracking unseen leviathans in the gloom.

Beyond the concourse rise the **inner tiers** of Draumhavn:

- The **Cliffforges**, where metal and tide meet—blacksmiths and shipwrights working side-by-side.
    
- The **Saltspire**, a crystalline tower where governance and divination entwine.
    
- The **Sanctuary of Leviathan**, built upon a natural stone rise that breaks through the sea dome like a fang, where elemental rites are held.
    

Yet for all its glory, Draumhavn feels taut. Watchful. As if it knows what stirs in the deep.

And then, as if to confirm it, a dull tremor passes beneath your feet—**just once**. Barely perceptible. But real.

Far below, **something** stirs in the **Abyss**.
